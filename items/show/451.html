In the year 2145, the world had progressed to an era of remarkable technological advancements. Humanity had successfully developed a brain-computer interface (BCI), which allowed direct communication between the human brain and computers. This revolutionary invention, known as Neuralink, had transformed the way humans interacted with technology and each other. People could now share their thoughts and emotions instantaneously, bridging geographical barriers and creating a more interconnected society. Dr. Evelyn Clarke, a renowned neuroscientist, was among the first group of researchers who had volunteered to implant the Neuralink device. As a pioneer in her field, she had dedicated her life to understanding the human brain and its potential. Little did she know that this cutting-edge technology would soon lead her to uncover the darkest secrets of the human mind. The story begins when Dr. Clarke receives an anonymous email containing a cryptic message that reads, "Beyond the neural horizon lies the truth." Intrigued, she embarks on a journey to unravel the mystery behind this message. Her quest leads her to a secluded underground laboratory, where she discovers a group of renegade scientists who have been working on a top-secret project. This shadowy organization, known as the NeuroCognitive Institute (NCI), has been conducting highly classified experiments on the human brain. They believe that the Neuralink device can be used to unlock hidden psychic abilities, such as telepathy and telekinesis, which could change the course of human history. Dr. Clarke, feeling a sense of responsibility to protect the world from the potential misuse of such technology, decides to infiltrate the NCI and sabotage their sinister plans. Along the way, she encounters several allies who share her vision of a world where technology serves humanity, rather than controlling it. Among her newfound friends is Jack, a former NCI researcher who had grown disillusioned with the organization's goals. Jack, who possesses a unique ability to control electronic devices using his mind, joins Dr. Clarke in her mission to expose the NCI's dark secrets. The duo embarks on a thrilling adventure, evading the NCI's relentless pursuit and racing against time to prevent the organization from achieving its nefarious objectives. As they delve deeper into the NCI's operations, they uncover a chilling truth: the NCI's leaders plan to use the Neuralink technology to create a new world order, where a select group of individuals with enhanced psychic abilities would control the masses, enslaving humanity under a regime of absolute power. With the odds stacked against them, Dr. Clarke and Jack must rely on their wits and their newfound psychic abilities to foil the NCI's plans. As they confront the organization's leaders in a climactic battle of wills, they discover that the key to victory lies not in their newfound powers, but in the strength of their unwavering conviction to protect the world from tyranny. In a final, desperate attempt to stop the NCI's leaders, Dr. Clarke and Jack engage in a telepathic duel, utilizing their psychic abilities to overload the Neuralink devices implanted in the villains' brains. The resulting explosion destroys the NCI's headquarters and puts an end to their sinister schemes. In the aftermath of their victory, Dr. Clarke and Jack, along with their allies, dedicate themselves to ensuring that the Neuralink technology is used for the betterment of mankind. They establish a new organization, the Cognitive Enlightenment Coalition (CEC), which works tirelessly to promote responsible research and development of neural technologies. As the story reaches its conclusion, Dr. Clarke reflects on her journey and the sacrifices she has made to protect the world from the dangers of unchecked technological advancement. She realizes that the true power of the human mind lies not in its potential to control the world around it, but in its capacity for empathy, compassion, and selflessness. The cryptic message that had set her on this path now holds a deeper meaning for Dr. Clarke. "Beyond the neural horizon," she thinks, "lies not only the truth, but the responsibility to ensure that our greatest achievements serve the greater good." And so, with a renewed sense of purpose, Dr. Clarke and her allies continue their mission to protect humanity from the perils of its own technological prowess. In a world where the boundaries between man and machine are increasingly blurred, they stand as a beacon of hope, reminding us that the power to shape our destiny lies not in the cold, unfeeling grasp of technology, but in the warmth and wisdom of the human heart.

In the early 15th century, in the small coastal town of Porto Vittoria, there lived a young man named Marco Vittorio. He was an apprentice to a renowned shipwright named Giovanni, who was well-known for building the finest ships in the region. The young and ambitious Marco was always eager to learn and his passion for the sea was boundless. His dreams were to one day build his own ship, sail the seas, and discover new lands. One day, while Marco was walking along the beach, he found a chest washed ashore by the tide. Curiosity piqued, he opened it and discovered a map. The map was old and worn, but it was clear that it depicted the coast of a land unknown to him. Marco believed that this was the key to his dreams and that the map led to a land untouched by man. Marco showed the map to Giovanni, who immediately recognized the potential it held. He advised Marco to gather a crew and set sail, as the map could lead them to great fortune and fame. Marco took Giovanni's words to heart and began assembling a crew of the bravest and most skilled sailors he could find. Together, they named themselves the Valiant Hearts, and their journey began. As the Valiant Hearts prepared for their voyage, whispers of their expedition reached the ears of the Duke of Porto Vittoria. The Duke, who was known to be a greedy and ruthless ruler, saw the potential of the Valiant Hearts' journey for his own gain. He ordered his men to capture the crew and seize the map, believing it held the key to untold wealth and power. The Duke's men arrived at the shipyard just as Marco and the crew were about to set sail. A fierce battle ensued, but the Valiant Hearts valiantly defended their ship and their dreams. In the midst of the chaos, Marco managed to hide the map, which he now wore as a pendant around his neck. With the map safely secured, the Valiant Hearts managed to escape and set sail, leaving the Duke's men and the town of Porto Vittoria behind them. The Valiant Hearts sailed for months, braving storms, treacherous waters, and the ever-present threat of pirates. They held on to their dreams, and their courage and determination only grew stronger with each passing day. During their journey, they encountered a mysterious island, which appeared to be the destination marked on the map. As they approached the island, they noticed a lush, green forest that seemed to be calling out to them. The crew decided to explore the island and see if it held the treasure they sought. As they ventured deeper into the forest, they stumbled upon a hidden village, which was inhabited by a peaceful and welcoming tribe. The tribe, known as the Guardians of the Green Forest, had lived on the island for centuries, and they welcomed the Valiant Hearts with open arms. The Guardians explained that they had been protecting a sacred treasure, which was the source of their prosperity and happiness. This treasure was a magical spring, which had the power to grant eternal youth and health to anyone who drank from it. The Guardians believed that this treasure should not be shared with the outside world, as it could lead to greed, power struggles, and the destruction of their island paradise. Marco and the Valiant Hearts were initially hesitant to accept the Guardians' offer, fearing that they would be seen as thieves and outcasts if they returned to their homeland empty-handed. However, the Guardians assured them that the true treasure was the bond they had formed with the tribe and the lessons they had learned about the importance of community, unity, and selflessness. As the Valiant Hearts spent more time with the Guardians, they realized that the treasure they sought was not the material wealth that they had originally set out to find, but rather the wisdom, friendship, and love that they had discovered on the island. The Duke of Porto Vittoria, who had been tracking the Valiant Hearts' voyage, eventually arrived at the island with his men, intent on seizing the magical spring for himself. He offered the Guardians a great fortune in exchange for the spring, but they refused, knowing that the Duke's greed would only lead to their ruin. The Duke, enraged by the Guardians' defiance, ordered his men to capture the spring and destroy the village. As the Guardians and the Valiant Hearts fought valiantly to protect their newfound home, the Duke's men managed to steal the spring and escape the island. With the magical spring gone, the Guardians and the Valiant Hearts mourned their loss, but they knew that their friendship and the lessons they had learned were the true treasure. They decided to leave the island and return to their homeland, where they would share their experiences and the wisdom they had gained with others. As the Valiant Hearts sailed back to Porto Vittoria, they vowed to use their newfound knowledge to create a world where love, unity, and selflessness were valued above all else. They knew that their journey had been filled with trials and tribulations, but they also knew that they had discovered something far more valuable than any material treasure. When the Valiant Hearts finally returned to Porto Vittoria, they found that the Duke had used the magical spring to amass great power and wealth, but his greed and corruption had turned the once-prosperous town into a place of darkness and despair. The people of Porto Vittoria, who had once revered the Duke, now despised him and yearned for the days when their town was a beacon of hope and unity. Marco and the Valiant Hearts, along with the Guardians who had chosen to accompany them, rallied the people of Porto Vittoria and led a rebellion against the tyrannical Duke. Despite the overwhelming odds stacked against them, the Valiant Hearts and the people of Porto Vittoria fought valiantly for their freedom, and their courage and unity ultimately led to the Duke's downfall. In the aftermath of their victory, the people of Porto Vittoria vowed to honor the Valiant Hearts and the lessons they had learned by building a new society based on love, unity, and selflessness. They named their town Valiant Hearts Bay, and it soon became a thriving community that was known far and wide for its kindness, generosity, and sense of community. As for Marco and the crew, they continued to sail the seas, sharing their story and the wisdom they had gained with others. They knew that their journey had been filled with trials and tribulations, but they also knew that they had discovered something far more valuable than any material treasure. The Chronicles of Valiant Hearts is a tale of heroism, sacrifice, and the power of the human spirit to overcome adversity and forge a better world. It is a reminder that the true treasure in life is not the material wealth we accumulate, but rather the love, friendship, and wisdom that we share with others.

In the heart of the dense and mysterious Amazon rainforest, a group of four friends embarked on a journey of adventure, courage, and camaraderie. Known as the "Amazon Four", the group consisted of Jake, a seasoned explorer; Sarah, an archaeologist; Max, a skilled survivalist; and Lily, an intrepid adventurer. Their mission was to discover and retrieve the Lost Relic, a legendary artifact believed to possess incredible power. The journey began with the team flying into Manaus, Brazil, where they gathered supplies and equipment, and hired a local guide, Carlos, who was familiar with the rainforest. As they ventured deeper into the heart of the Amazon, the team faced numerous obstacles, including treacherous terrain, deadly creatures, and a relentless downpour that threatened to drown them. One day, they came across a village inhabited by the indigenous people of the Amazon, who shared with them an ancient prophecy. The prophecy spoke of a great evil that had been sealed away by the ancient gods, hidden within a sacred temple guarded by a powerful relic. The prophecy also foretold that the relic would only reveal itself to the pure of heart, and only then could it be retrieved. Driven by the prospect of finding the Lost Relic and uncovering its secrets, the Amazon Four pressed onward. After several days of navigating through dense jungles, they stumbled upon a hidden entrance to a vast underground network of caverns. They soon discovered that these caverns were the remains of an ancient civilization that had once ruled the Amazon. As they ventured further into the caverns, they encountered a series of traps and puzzles that tested their wit, strength, and teamwork. They successfully overcame each challenge, learning more about the ancient civilization and their reverence for the Lost Relic. At the end of the cavern, they found themselves in front of a massive door adorned with intricate carvings. As they stared at the door in awe, the prophecy came to mind, and Sarah, the archaeologist, stepped forward. She uttered the sacred words spoken in the ancient language, and the door slowly creaked open, revealing a hidden chamber. Inside the chamber, they found the Lost Relic: a golden staff that radiated with an ethereal glow. As they approached it, the staff began to pulsate, and a voice boomed, warning them of the consequences of attempting to wield its power. Undeterred, Jake, the seasoned explorer, reached out to grasp the staff, but suddenly, a shadowy figure emerged from the darkness. The figure introduced himself as Ragnar, a powerful sorcerer who had been trapped within the temple for centuries. Ragnar had used the Lost Relic to amass power, but in the process, he had unleashed a terrible curse that threatened the entire world. The ancient gods had imprisoned him within the temple, sealing away the Lost Relic to prevent him from regaining his power. Unwilling to let the Lost Relic fall into the hands of a malevolent sorcerer, the Amazon Four banded together to defeat Ragnar. Through a combination of cunning, teamwork, and sheer determination, they were able to outwit and overcome Ragnar's dark magic. As Ragnar's power waned, the Lost Relic began to pulse even more intensely. In a final, desperate attempt, Ragnar summoned a vortex of dark energy that threatened to consume the entire chamber. With no time to spare, the Amazon Four worked together to lift the Lost Relic from its pedestal, channeling its power to defeat Ragnar once and for all. As the vortex began to dissipate, the Lost Relic pulsed one last time before disappearing in a brilliant flash of light. The Amazon Four found themselves back in the cavern, the golden staff now gone. As they emerged from the caverns, they realized that the ancient prophecy had come true. The Lost Relic had been destroyed, and with it, the power that had once sealed away Ragnar. The Amazon Four returned to the village, where they were hailed as heroes. The villagers celebrated their victory, and the Amazon Four knew that they had not only saved the world from a terrible fate but had also secured their place in history as the heroes who had conquered the Lost Relic. As they looked back on their incredible journey, they knew that their adventure had come to an end, but the bonds of friendship and camaraderage they had formed would last a lifetime. And as they embarked on their journey home, they knew that the story of the Amazon Four and their quest for the Lost Relic would be passed down through generations, serving as a testament to the power of friendship, courage, and the indomitable human spirit.

REPORT

OF THE

CONVENTION

OF THE

COLOURED POPULATION,

HELD AT

DRUMMONDVILLE, AUG., 1847.

TORONTO:

PRINTED AT THE BANNER OFFICE, YONGE STREET.

1847.

THE

COLOURED POPULATION OF CANADA .

It had long been a matter of regret that there did not exist in Canada,any regularly organised Society, to which the individuals who escape from the slavery of the United States might look for assistance and for advice as to their future guidance. Such an Institution has become absolutely essential to the welfare of the coloured population. The friends of the long oppressed African race, residing in Canada, Great Britain, and the United Stales, are ever willing to assist those who escape from the land of bondage with aid to settle in their new abode, and to gain their bread by lawful industry. But in order that this may be done with confidence, it is necessary that the management and distribution should be placed under such, as would be able and faithful guardians of what was committed to them. The increasing number of the coloured population in Canada, has for several years attracted the attention of the philanthropists in Great Britain and the United States, and missions have been undertaken by various individuals to these countries, to procure assistance for them. With the money realized by one of these missions the settlement of Dawn, Sydenham, Western Canada, was formed several years ago. Large sums of money and quantities of clothing have since been received from Great Britain and the United States, but it is not known how they

4

have been disposed of. Whether there has been faithful management or not, the mass of the coloured population, for whose behoof these collections were made, are generally dissatisfied, as no satisfactory account of these transactions had ever been published.

The first step taken to bring these interests into a regular business-like form, was the organization of a Society some months ago, entitled "THE BRITISH AMERICAN ANTI-SLAVERY SOCIETY." Its head quarters are at Toronto, but it is intended that it shall have branches aIl over the Province. This Society has met with the approbation of the coloured population of Toronto, and of many other friends. But something more than this was required to raise up a central power, to which the self-emancipated slave might look for protection and assistance—the distressed meet with sympathy and support in the hour of need—the interests of the coloured population be promoted, and their persons protected by prompt appeals to the justice of British law. With a view to carry out these important objects, it was deemed advisable to hold a General Convention of the coloured population of the Province. On the 7th of June last an address was extensively circulated from Toronto, and published in several newspapers, inviting a Convention to be held in Drummondville, on the 2nd day of August and subsequent days.

THE CONVENTION.

SESSION I.

In consequence of the said call, the Convention met at Drummondville, on Monday, the 2nd day of August, 1847, in the African Methodist Episcopal Chapel.

The House was opened with singing, and prayer by the Rev. N. C. W. Cannon.

Rev. Mr. Cannon was unanimously appointed President, and Mr. Charles Freeman, Secretary, pro tem.

The call of the Convention having been read; on motion of Mr. Smallwood, the Delegates present were requested to present their credentials, when the following gentlemen took their seats, viz. :—

Mr. George Ramsay, - - - - - - - Harwich.

Rev. Alexander Elmsley, - - - - - - "

Mr. J. B. Lindsay, - - - - - - - "

Rev. Alexander Broadwales, - - - - - "

Mr. Benjamin Russell, - - - - - - Drummondville.

Mr. J. Taylor, - - - - - - - - - "

UNITED STATES.

Mr. John Watson, - - - " " "

Rev. John Liles, - - - Syracuse, Onandago Co., N.Y.

On motion, a Committee of three was appointed by the Chairman, consisting of Messrs. Smallwood, Dunlop, and Liles, to nominate officers for the Convention. The Committee having retired a short time, returned and reported the following persons as suitable for officers of the Convention, viz. :—

Rev. N. C. W. Cannon, - - - President.

Rev. John Liles, - - - - - Vice-President.

Mr. Charles Freeman, - - - Secretary.

Mr. J. W. Stringer, - - - - Assistant Secretary.

The meeting having approved of the Report, also appointed the following Committees :—

ON BUSINESS—Messrs. Smallwood, Ramsay, Watson, Counter, and J. T. Henson.

ON RULES—Messrs. Liles, Dunlop, and Couuter,

ON FINANCE—Messrs. Warren, Counter, and Smallwood.

Convention adjourned to 9 o'clock next morning.

SESSION II.

TUESDAY, August 3rd—9 A.M.

House opened with praise, and prayer by the Rev. Mr. Liles.

7

A letter was read from Mr. D. Jenkins, Columbus, Ohio, on the importance of the duties of the Convention.

A letter was also read from Dawn, concerning the illegal election of one of the Delegates from that place.

Both letters were laid on the table for after consideration.

On motion of Mr. Dunlop, it was

Resolved,—That the members of this Convention declare to the world that they have met for no sectarian purpose whatever—that LIBERTY is their motto, and TRUTH their standard.

Adjourned till 3 o'clock in the afternoon.

—

SESSION III.

TUESDAY, August 3rd—3 P.M.

House opened as usual with singing and prayer.

The Business Committee reported—that they would recommend to the consideration of the Convention the latter part of the call which relates to the moral, religious, and social condition of their people, and the best means for their improvement.

Report laid on the table.

In accordance therewith, it was then

Resolved,—That means should be taken to ascertain, as far as possible, what is the condition of our people, in order that we may be enabled to devise means for their elevation.

Resolution laid on the table.

Resolved,—That the best mode for improving the moral, religious, and social condition of our people in Canada is,

8

by means of Common School Education, and the acquirement of useful knowledge.

Resolved,—That the mind of the House be taken, whether information should not be received, as to the disposal of the money and clothing sent by the friends of the coloured people in Canada for their benefit, and who had participated in it.

One case was mentioned, but it happened before the present Agents commenced their operations.

Moved by Mr. Smallwood, and

Resolved,—That if the monies, clothing, and other articles contributed in the United States and Great Britain had been equitably and judiciously divided, our condition would have been greatly better than it now is.

Laid on the table .

Moved by Mr. Smallwood, seconded by Mr. Cannon,—

Resolved,—That this Convention is satisfied that frauds have been committed upon the people of colour in Canada, in regard to monies, clothing, and other articles sent from the United States and elsewhere.—Laid on the table.

Adjourned till next day.

—

SESSION IV.

WEDNESDAY, August 4th,—9 A M.

Convention met, and opened in the usual manner.

Rev. Josiah Henson presented his credentials, and took his seat as Delegate from Dawn.

After a long discussion in regard to the order of the day, and other minor matters, the Rev. J. Henson moved the following resolution :—

"Whereas it is our duty to contend for truth and justice,

9

if any gentleman brings a charge against the character of a man, whether present or absent, and cannot substantiate the fact by good proof, he is chargeable with immorality, and is unworthy of public confidence."

Laid on the table for further consideration.

Mr. Ramsay then moved—"That the Convention ascertain as far as possible, whether the Dawn institution belongs to a Joint Stock Company, or whether it belongs to the People of Colour in Canada generally."—Laid over to the afternoon session.

The motion by Mr. Smallwood yesterday, respecting the supplies from abroad, was called up for adoption, and laid over till the afternoon session.

The Convention then adjourned.

—

SESSION V.

WEDNESDAY, August 4th—3 P.M.

The Convention met—the President in the Chair.

Mr. Smallwood's resolution was again brought up, and a long discussion took place. The motion was supported by Messrs. Smallwood, Dunlop, Warren, Watson, Counter, Liles, and Blanknort; and opposed by Mr. J. Henson. On division it was carried, viz. :—Yeas, Messrs. Dunlop, Liles, Smallwood, Freeman, Cannon, Warren, Ramsay, Ferris, Watson, Counter, Elmsley, Stringer, and J. T. Henson—13. Nay, Mr. J. Henson—1.

The motion adopted was as follows :—

Resolved,—That this Convention declares their belief that frauds have been practised upon the People of Colour in Canada, in regard to monies, clothing, and other articles given by friends in the United States and elsewhere for our benefit.

The Convention then adjourned to Thursday.

10

SESSION VI.

THURSDAY, 5th August—9 A.M.

The Convention met at 9 o'clock A.M., and was opened as usual by prayer and praise.

Mr. Henson's resolution was taken up, as already given, viz. :— " Whereas it is our duty to contend for truth and justice—if any gentleman brings a charge against the character of a man, whether present or absent, and cannot substantiate the fact by good proof, he shall be charged with immorality, and is not worthy of public confidence."

The following amendment was then offered by Mr. Smallwood, and adopted by the meeting :—

"Whereas it is our duty to contend for truth and justice, and if any gentleman brings a charge against the character of any man, whether present or absent, whose public acts are not shrouded in mystery, thereby creating suspicion against his character, which he cannot substantiate by good proof, he shall be charged with immorality, and be held unworthy of public confidence."

The following questions were then put to Mr. J. Henson by the meeting, viz. :—

Question—For whom do you act as Agent?

Answer—I am Agent for the British American Institute in Dawn, Canada West.

Q.—What is its object?

A.—It is an Institntion situated on Sydenham River—a river navigable for boats of 300 tons—land fertile—and it is for any young men, to be educated at their own expense.

Q.—To whom are you amenable?

A.—To the Executive Committee.

Q.—To whom are the Executive Committee amenable?

A.—To the Trustees.

Q.—To whom are the Trustees amenable?

11

A.—To the donors. I have collected fifteen hundred dollars since the 1st March last, in hard cash.

Mr. Ramsay's motion was then brought forward:—

Resolved,—That the Convention ascertain as far as possible whether the Dawn Institution is a Joint Stock Company, or an institution for the People of Colour in Canada generally.—Consideration deferred.

Convention adjourned.

—

SESSION VII.

THURSDAY, August 5th—3 P.M.

Mr. Ramsay's motion was taken up:—

Resolved,—That the Dawn Institution belongs to the People of Colour in Canada generally.

Vote taken, and stood as follows:—Yeas, Messrs. Cannon, Smallwood, Ramsay, Freeman, Dunlop, Warren, J. Henson, J. S . Henson, Elmsley, Ferris, Watson, Liles, Stringer, Counter, Taylor, Russell—16. Nay—none.

It was then

Resolved,—That whereas the British American Institute in the Township of Dawn, Canada West, is not conducted to the satisfaction of the People of Colour in this Province, a Committee of five members of this Convention be appointed to audit the books and papers of said Institution—the said Committee to make an exact report of the result of their investigation to the next meeting of the People's Convention, to be held at Drummondville, in August, 1848.

It was farther

Resolved,—That the said Committee act as a Joint Committee in the management of the Institution at Dawn, and that they shall be responsible to the Convention.

12

The following Committee was then named by the Chair —two from the United States, and three from Canada West:—Rev. John Liles, Messrs. T. W. Stringer, E . B. Dunlop, J. B. Lindsay, and J. T. Henson.

The following proposition, previously brought forward, was then agreed to:—

Resolved,—That if the monies, clothing, and other articles sent from the United States and elsewhere, had been equitably and judiciously distributed among the people of colour in Canada, our condition would have been greatly superior to what it now is.

Resolved,—That the Convention is satisfied that the Agents into whose hands these donations by the friends of humanity have fallen, have utterly failed to carry out the intentions of the benevolent donors.

Resolved,—That the Convention recognises the British American Anti-Slavery Society in the City of Toronto, and its auxiliaries, as the only regularly organized association of the kind in Canada, and recommend it to the support of the friends of the coloured race.

Resolved,—That Agents shall be appointed in convenient places in the Province, who shall have power to appoint sub-Agents for the distribution in the interior, of such goods as may be entrusted to them—these sub-Agents to be responsible to the chief Agents, who shall be responsible to an Executive Committee, and the Executive Committee to the British American Anti-Slavery Society.

Resolved,—That it shall be the duty of the Executive Committee to see that all the goods and chattels given for the benefit of fugitive slaves be put into the hands of their

13

Agents, that they may be faithfully distributed among the proper objects of charity; and the said Agents shall report the same to an Auxiliary Anti-Slavery Society, of which each Agent must be a member. Each Auxiliary Society shall report all their transactions at least once a year to the Parent Society, which shall publish annually a full account of all the receipts and disbursements of the Society, in money or any kind of articles, with an account of what then remains on hand.

Resolved,—That the British American Anti-Slavery Society be called together, in order that the words "Prudential Committee," be withdrawn from the Constitution, and "Executive Committee" be substituted, and the said Committee shall have power to appoint Agents.

Resolved,—That all coloured people in Canada be recommended to become freeholders as soon as they have an opportunity of doing so, as a legitimate means of increasing their influence in the community.

Resolved,—That this Convention recommend to all their brethren throughout the Province to tolerate nothing but truth in their intercourse with the world—to be quiet and peaceable subjects—and to avoid all cause of censure or reproach from those around them.

Resolved:—That the Executive Committee shall cause an oath of fidelity to be taken by the officers of the Society, if they shall deem it necessary.

The Convention then adjourned.

14

SESSION VIII.

FRIDAY, August 6th—9 A.M.

Convention met, and was opened as usual.

Moved by Rev. Mr. Cannon, seconded by Mr. Stringer,—

Resolved,—

That the Executive Committee be instructed to take every means of ascertaining the names of the Agents who have been employed in collecting money, &c., for the coloured people of Canada, to ascertain the amount, and report the same, that full information may be published in the Minutes and to the Convention of 1848.

Resolved,—That the Convention feels deeply grateful for the benevolence of their many kind friends. They desire to cast their care upon God, who has hitherto cared for and protected them.

On motion of Mr. J. T. Henson, it was

Resolved:—That the coloured people of Canada are not in the degraded state in which they have been represented by some of the pretended Agents who have gone out.

On the report of the Business Committee being brought up, the following motions were passed:—

Resolved,—That we first examine what the condition of our people really is, and then we shall be enabled to devise means for their elevation.

Resolved.—That among the best means for improving the moral, religious, and social condition of our people are—Common and Industrial School education, and instruction in the truths of the Word of God.

15

Resolved,—That the Convention recommend the British American Anti-Slavery Society to use their utmost exertions to establish an Anti-Slavery Paper in the City of Toronto.

Resolved,—That the Delegates who shall attend the Convention of 1848, be requested to bring accurate accounts, as far as possible, of the Schools and other properly belonging to their constituents.

Resolved,—That the letter from Dawn be handed over to the Auditing Committee.

Two additional members were then voted to form part of the Auditing Committee —one from the United Slates and one from this Province —viz.: Messrs. W. P. Newman, and G. Ramsay.

Mr. J . Henson then rose and proceeded to satisfy the Convention in regard to his agency for the British American Institute. He handed his book to the Secretary, with an account of his intromissions since the 1st March last. This was read over to the Convention, and it showed clearly that Mr. Henson had faithfully discharged his duty to the institution.

It was then

Resolved,—That the Convention is satisfied with the conduct of Mr. Henson, so far as they have heard from him.

Convention then adjourned.

SESSION IX.

FRIDAY, August 6th—3 P.M.

Met at 3 o'clock P.M., and opened as usual.

Resolved,—That the Auditing Committee be instructed to memorialize the Governor General and the Provincial Parliament, praying them not to incorporate the Dawn Institution in its present state.

Resolved,—That these minutes be printed, and that each copy shall not exceed 4 1/2 d.—that 1000 copies be printed, or 1500, if necessary, and that the proceeds shall go to defray the expenses of the next Convention.

Resolved,—That the Convention meet again on the 15th day of August, 1848, at Drummondville, Canada West, under her Majesty's protection.

Adjourned.

N.C.W. CANNON, President,

CHARLES FREEMAN, Secretary,

T. W. STRINGER, Asst. Sec'y.

The Collective lived in a massive underground facility, where they had access to the resources they needed to survive. The facility was equipped with state-of-the-art technology that allowed them to monitor the outside world and communicate with the scattered remnants of humanity. One day, Dr. Reeves received a message from a small group of survivors who had discovered a hidden underground city, untouched by the environmental disaster that had ravaged the surface. This city, known as Haven, was located far beneath the Earth's surface and was believed to have been built by a long-lost civilization. Dr. Reeves and a team of experts were sent to investigate Haven, hoping to find the knowledge and technology needed to save the world. Upon arriving, they discovered a vast library filled with ancient scrolls, tablets, and holographic archives. These artifacts contained the collective wisdom of the civilization that had built Haven, and they held the key to unlocking the secrets of the world's destruction. As the Collective delved deeper into the mysteries of Haven, they uncovered a dark truth. The Collective was faced with a difficult choice. Dr. Reeves, a firm believer in the potential for redemption, argued that they should try to save their home planet.
